# Three Hours
#
# The island at night, looking north, from the slope below the house. Three hours of it, all at once.
#
# The rule of this painting: only what moves is painted. A stroke is the way something went during the three hours.
# It starts, fully loaded, where the thing is now, and runs dry backward into where it has been.
# What kept still gets no paint: the house, the hill, the two jackdaws asleep on the chimney are bare canvas.
# Air and water are never still, so they get a thin wash.
#
# Every line below was written by hand, one stroke at a time, by Claude. Three tools were used besides the brush:
# a string pinned at the Pole Star (the sky turns about that point, so each star's trail is an arc on that string),
# a straight pull of the brush for light, and paper masks to keep paint off what stood still.
